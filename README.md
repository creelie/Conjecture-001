# Maximal sublattices of finite semidistributive lattices: the complement need not be an interval

Adams, Freese, Nation and Schmid, *Maximal sublattices and Frattini sublattices of bounded lattices*,
J. Austral. Math. Soc. Ser. A 63 (1997), 110–127,
[doi:10.1017/S1446788700000355](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700000355), proved that in a finite
bounded lattice the complement of a maximal sublattice is an interval [v, u] with u ∈ CM(v) and
v ∈ CJ(u), and asked: "It is natural to ask whether this characterization extends to finite
semidistributive lattices. We do not know." Adaricheva, Mata, Silberger and Zamojska-Dzienio,
*Conjecture on maximal sublattices of finite semidistributive lattices and beyond*, Discrete Math.
Theor. Comput. Sci. 28:2 (2026), [doi:10.46298/dmtcs.16374](https://doi.org/10.46298/dmtcs.16374)
([arXiv:2507.22682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22682)), stated the expected answer as their
Hypothesis 1: the complements of maximal sublattices of finite semidistributive lattices are intervals.

**The answer is no.** This repository gives a semidistributive lattice L with 26 elements and a
maximal sublattice D whose complement has two minimal and two maximal elements, so it is not an
interval, and is not even order convex.

| | |
|---|---|
| K | the 14-element semidistributive lattice with covering pairs 0≺1, 0≺2, 1≺3, 1≺4, 2≺4, 2≺7, 3≺5, 3≺6, 4≺9, 5≺10, 6≺8, 6≺9, 7≺12, 8≺10, 8≺11, 9≺12, 10≺13, 11≺13, 12≺11 |
| L | the sublattice of K × K generated by the diagonal and the pair (1, 3); equivalently the pairs (x, y) with x ≤ y ≤ φ(x), where φ = (0, 6, 7, 8, 12, 10, 8, 7, 8, 11, 10, 11, 11, 13) |
| D | the diagonal {(x, x)}, a copy of K and a maximal sublattice of L |
| C = L ∖ D | (1,3), (1,6), (2,7), (3,6), (3,8), (4,9), (4,12), (5,10), (6,8), (9,11), (9,12), (12,11) |
| minimal elements of C | (1,3) and (2,7), whose meet (0,0) lies in D |
| maximal elements of C | (5,10) and (12,11) |

Maximality of D comes from one cycle: joining and meeting with diagonal elements leads from any
pair of C through all twelve of them, e.g. (1,3) ∨ (2,2) = (4,9), (4,9) ∧ (10,10) = (1,6), …,
(4,12) ∧ (3,3) = (1,3). K is not bounded (it has the D-cycle 6 D 7 D 8 D 6); by the theorem of
Adams, Freese, Nation and Schmid no bounded lattice can be a counterexample. By the author's earlier exhaustive check
([doi:10.5281/zenodo.23343145](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23343145), Theorem E) every
semidistributive lattice with at most 14 elements satisfies Hypothesis 1, so the least size of a
counterexample is between 15 and 26.

## What is proved, and how

The paper (`preprint/main.tex`, built into `dist/`) proves everything by hand. The Lean 4 proof
with Mathlib (v4.34.1) states the negation of Hypothesis 1 over all finite lattices:

```lean
theorem not_complement_interval :
    ¬∀ (α : Type) [Lattice α] [Finite α],
      (∀ a b c : α, a ⊔ b = a ⊔ c → a ⊔ b = a ⊔ (b ⊓ c)) →
      (∀ a b c : α, a ⊓ b = a ⊓ c → a ⊓ b = a ⊓ (b ⊔ c)) →
      ∀ M : Sublattice α, IsCoatom M → ∃ u v : α, ∀ x : α, x ∉ M ↔ u ≤ x ∧ x ≤ v
```

| Statement | Lean theorem |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis 1 fails | `H1.not_complement_interval` |
| K and L are semidistributive | `H1.K.sdSup`, `H1.K.sdInf`, `H1.L.sdSup`, `H1.L.sdInf` |
| L embeds in K × K | `H1.L.emb_injective`, `H1.L.emb_sup`, `H1.L.emb_inf` |
| D is a maximal proper sublattice | `H1.L.isCoatom_D` |
| C has no least element, so it is not an interval | `H1.L.no_least`, `H1.L.not_interval` |

All proofs are checked by the kernel (`decide +kernel` and explicit derivations, no
`native_decide`); `lean/Check.lean` prints the axioms of ten theorems, and
`scripts/audit_axioms.py` checks that only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` occur
(`verification/axioms.txt`). `lean/H1/Counterexample.lean` is generated from the covering pairs of K
by `scripts/gen_lean.py`.

Independent checks outside Lean (`scripts/run_all.sh`):

- `verification/c/h1check.c`: rebuilds K and L, checks both semidistributive laws on all triples,
  the maximality of D by generating L from D and each element of C, the minimal and maximal
  elements of C, the D-cycle and the self-duality of K; it also lists the 46 sublattices of K × K
  generated by the diagonal and one further pair (`data/single_pair_sizes.txt`).
- `verification/python/check_paper.py`: reads the tables, the pair list, the cycle (4.1) and the
  footnote out of `preprint/main.tex` and recomputes every number.
- `verification/python/single_pair_search.py`: decides by SAT (python-sat), for each of the 46
  sublattices generated by the diagonal and one pair, whether some maximal sublattice has a
  complement that is not an interval. Exactly eight do, of sizes 26, 26, 47, 47, 47, 47, 56, 56; the
  two with 26 elements are L and its transpose. This is the search that found the example.

## Layout

```
lean/                       Lean 4 + Mathlib (v4.34.1)
  H1/Counterexample.lean    K, L, D and the theorems above
  Check.lean                axiom audit
verification/c/             h1check.c
verification/python/        check_paper.py, single_pair_search.py
verification/axioms.txt     output of Check.lean
data/                       single_pair_sizes.txt
preprint/                   the paper (amsart) and its TikZ figures
dist/                       the paper as PDF, as LaTeX with PNG figures, and as an arXiv tarball
scripts/                    gen_lean.py, gen_figures.py, build_paper.sh, run_all.sh, audit_axioms.py
PRIORITY-CHECK.md           the dated check that the question was still open
```

To reproduce: `scripts/run_all.sh` (C compiler, Python 3 with python-sat), `cd lean && lake exe
cache get && lake build && lake env lean Check.lean`, and `scripts/build_paper.sh` (TeX Live).
The workflow `.github/workflows/verify.yml` runs all three on every pull request.
